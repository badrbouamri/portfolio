## Context

Two-month end-of-studies internship (PFA) at AIC Métallurgie (Delta Holding group), in Kénitra — a metal-forming site. The assigned scope covered the fleet of bending and rolling machines, whose availability directly determines whether production deadlines are met.

## Problem

The fleet of bending and rolling machines suffered recurring failures, causing unplanned production stoppages. The site had neither a structured analysis of these failures nor a formalised maintenance plan for this machine fleet.

## Objectives

- Identify the causes of recurring failures on the bending and rolling machines
- Calculate the rolling and unrolling forces specific to these machines
- Formalise a preventive and corrective maintenance plan
- Formulate recommendations to improve fleet reliability and reduce production stoppages

## My role

I carried out the failure diagnosis and the rolling/unrolling force calculations on my own. I proposed the preventive and corrective maintenance plan and the reliability recommendations, handed over to the site's maintenance team for validation and implementation.

## Approach & methodology

**Failure data collection** — gathering the history of stoppages and failures on the fleet's bending and rolling machines.

**Recurring failure analysis** — mechanical diagnosis of the failure modes observed on these machines.

**Rolling and unrolling force calculation** — sizing of the forces applied during bending and rolling operations, to objectively quantify the stresses the machine fleet is subjected to.

**Maintenance plan development** — building a preventive and corrective maintenance plan, and writing reliability-improvement recommendations.

## Tools & methods

`Mechanical diagnostics` `Preventive maintenance` `Corrective maintenance` `Rolling force calculation`

## Engineering analysis

The diagnosis covered the fleet's two rolling machines, DAVI and JAMMES-PROMESS, tracked over a two-month window (February-March). For each, the stoppage history was classified with an Ishikawa diagram, then weighted with a Pareto — not by failure frequency, but by each cause's actual share of cumulative downtime, which reorders the priorities: a rare but long incident (pump cavitation, 15% of DAVI's downtime) outweighs a frequent but short one (clogged oil exchanger, 4%).

On DAVI, three causes account for 54% of downtime: breakage of the sheet-guide stop (21%), circularity defects from roller wear (18%), and hydraulic-pump cavitation (15%). On JAMMES-PROMESS, the same circularity defect dominates (26%), followed by clamping-cylinder seizure (21%) and drive-variator overcurrent (17%). The causes retained as priorities group into six factor families (mechanical, electrical, electronic, material, human, parameter settings) — mechanical and hydraulic factors clearly dominate on both machines, which steered the maintenance plan toward reconditioning moving parts (rollers, cylinders, chains) rather than automation, a secondary factor over this period.

The reliability indicators point to a differentiated diagnosis rather than a simple ranking: DAVI fails less often (MTBF 32h) but keeps the line down longer per incident (MTTR 5.7h), for 84.9% availability; JAMMES-PROMESS is repaired faster (MTTR 3.6h) despite a shorter MTBF (25h), giving it higher availability (87.4%). This reading ruled out the initial hypothesis — applying the same maintenance plan to both machines — in favour of two distinct priorities: reducing DAVI's MTTR (critical spares in stock, fast changeover procedures) rather than its already-acceptable failure frequency, and reinforcing targeted preventive maintenance on JAMMES-PROMESS to extend its MTBF.

The internship's second axis was the analytical calculation of rolling forces, to objectively quantify the machines' real capacity against the parts they process. Method: starting from the sheet's equilibrium equations during rolling (central and side roller radii, tilt angle, material properties), a Matlab program solves for the force each roller exerts (side roller Q1, top roller Q2, bottom roller Q3) for a given set of parameters.

The result retained: sheet thickness is the factor that weighs most heavily on force (case 2, the thickest, generates the highest Q2 despite a large radius), and bend radius acts in the opposite direction — the smaller it is, the higher the force at equal thickness (case 4, a tight 375mm radius, produces a force nearly as high as case 2 despite a sheet half as thick). The hypothesis ruled out was sizing on thickness alone; case 4 shows a tight radius can by itself push a machine to its limits, even on a moderately thick sheet — hence the recommendation to always cross-check thickness against radius before committing a part to these rollers, rather than relying on either parameter alone.

## Challenges encountered

The available failure history covered only two months (February-March), which limits the statistical robustness of the calculated MTBF and MTTR — six events for DAVI, eight for JAMMES-PROMESS. Rather than presenting these indicators as fixed values, they were used as a relative comparison between the two machines (which fails less often, which gets repaired faster) — a more robust reading on a short sample than an isolated absolute value.

The rolling-force calculation required precise technical parameters (roller diameters, tilt angle, material mechanical properties) that weren't centralised in a single document; they had to be reconstructed from machine spec sheets and the material properties of the parts being processed, then fed into a Matlab model to get usable results.

Finally, DAVI and JAMMES-PROMESS rest on different mechanical architectures (roller count, clamping method, drive system): the same Ishikawa diagram wouldn't have made sense for both. Each machine was therefore diagnosed separately before extracting the six shared factor families, which doubled the classification work within a two-month internship.

## Solutions implemented

- Preventive and corrective maintenance plan for the fleet of bending and rolling machines
- Reliability recommendations for the machine fleet aimed at reducing production stoppages

## Results

This internship focused on diagnosis and formalising an action plan; its implementation and the measurement of its effect (availability rate, stoppage frequency) were handed over to the site's maintenance team after the internship ended.

## What I take from this

This internship taught me to prefer a relative comparison over an absolute value when the sample is short: over two months of data, contrasting the two machines' MTBF and MTTR said more than either indicator taken on its own. I also saw firsthand that ranking failures by frequency can be misleading — it's the share of downtime, not the number of occurrences, that should drive maintenance priorities.

The rolling-force calculation also confirmed an intuition that needed checking rather than assuming: thickness alone isn't enough to judge whether a part is within a machine's capacity — bend radius alone can tip the calculation. That kind of gap between intuition and calculation is, I think, what separates a solid diagnosis from a merely plausible one.
