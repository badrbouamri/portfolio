## Problem

A fixed-timer watering schedule ignores two pieces of information that are readily available: actual soil moisture and upcoming rainfall. As a result, a non-connected system either over-waters (wasting water) or under-waters (water stress), with no data available afterward to verify which.

## Objectives

- Automate irrigation triggering from a local measurement (soil moisture), rather than a fixed timer.
- Integrate a weather forecast (OpenWeather API) to avoid watering before expected rain.
- Enable remote supervision and control of the system.

## My role

A personal project carried out solo: architecture design, embedded firmware development, and setting up communication and remote supervision. No team split to document — the entire chain, from sensor to supervision, is this same individual work.

## Approach & methodology

The chosen architecture follows a simple chain, typical of this kind of IoT project:

1. **Local acquisition** — an ESP32 microcontroller continuously reads a soil moisture sensor (and, where relevant, other environmental readings).
2. **Local decision** — a moisture threshold triggers a pre-decision to water directly on the ESP32, without permanently depending on a network connection.
3. **Weather correction** — before executing the watering action, the system queries the OpenWeather API to check for imminent rain and adjusts or cancels the decision.
4. **Communication** — measurements and system status are reported via the **MQTT** protocol, chosen for its lightweight publish/subscribe model suited to resource-constrained devices, rather than classic HTTP polling, which is more costly in energy and network traffic.
5. **Remote supervision** — a dashboard makes it possible to track the system's status and history, and to intervene manually if needed.

## Engineering analysis

Two design choices deserve justification:

**MQTT rather than direct HTTP polling.** A battery-powered or power-constrained connected device benefits from minimising the number and duration of active network connections. MQTT's publish/subscribe model lets the ESP32 transmit only when an event warrants it (state change, significant reading), instead of continuously polling or being polled — reducing both power consumption and network load.

**Local decision before remote correction.** Keeping the moisture threshold directly on the ESP32 (rather than offloading everything to a server) ensures the system keeps working autonomously even during a network outage or when the weather API is unavailable — the weather data then refines a decision that remains functional without it.
